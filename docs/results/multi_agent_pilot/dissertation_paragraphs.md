# Dissertation Paragraphs: Multi-Agent Stage 2C Pilot

A small multi-agent pilot was used to test whether decomposing species classification into a classifier, acoustic reviewer, and final adjudicator improved the Stage 2C selected-proposal workflow. The pilot used the same 24 samples for both qwen3.6 and GPT-5.6 Sol, with fixed nearest-centre BatDetect2 proposal coordinates and label-safe centred crop images. The agents were not asked to localise or redraw bounding boxes, so the experiment isolated species classification and review behaviour.

For qwen3.6, the multi-agent workflow barely improved performance over the single-agent baseline on the same hard subset. Single-agent qwen3.6 achieved 0.000 accuracy, while the multi-agent version achieved 0.042 accuracy and 0.014 macro-F1. The reviewer/adjudicator also failed to provide useful uncertainty signalling: no cases were marked for human review despite the very low final accuracy. This indicates that role decomposition did not compensate for weak species-level acoustic discrimination in the underlying model.

GPT-5.6 Sol was substantially stronger than qwen3.6 on the same samples, achieving 0.542 accuracy and 0.488 macro-F1 in the multi-agent setting. However, this did not materially exceed GPT-5.6 Sol single-agent performance, which achieved 0.542 accuracy and 0.486 macro-F1 on the same subset. The GPT reviewer did produce some uncertainty signal, marking 7 of 24 cases for review, but the forced-choice accuracy was unchanged. These results suggest that stronger model capability, rather than multi-agent decomposition alone, drove the observed performance gain.

The multi-agent findings support a cautious human-in-the-loop framing. Multi-agent structures may be useful for triage, review prompts, and surfacing uncertainty, especially when paired with a stronger model. However, the present pilot does not show that classifier-reviewer-adjudicator decomposition improves forced-choice species accuracy by itself. For the dissertation, the result is best interpreted as evidence that agentic structure must be coupled with genuinely capable acoustic-visual models and robust uncertainty validation, rather than treated as a substitute for model competence.
